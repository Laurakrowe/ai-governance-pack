**Companion to:** Artificial Intelligence (AI) Policy

This guide is the employee-facing companion to the Organization\'s AI Policy. It explains, in plain terms, what you can and cannot do with AI tools at work. If there is ever a conflict between this guide and the AI Policy, the AI Policy governs.

**[1. PURPOSE]{.underline}**

This guide provides clear, practical rules for how Organization staff use AI tools in their daily work. The goal is simple: help you take advantage of AI to work faster and smarter, while keeping Organization and public information safe.

**[2. WHO THIS APPLIES TO]{.underline}**

All Organization employees, contractors, and volunteers who use Organization-issued accounts or devices. Note that some tools (such as Canva AI) are approved for staff use only \-- see the per-tool notes in Section 5.

**[3. WHAT YOU CAN DO]{.underline}**

- **Use approved AI tools for work tasks.** See Section 5 below for the current list of approved tools.

- **Use AI to help draft, summarize, brainstorm, and organize.** AI is a helpful assistant for getting started on documents, emails, presentations, and analysis.

- **Always review AI output before using it.** Read what the AI produces. Check it for accuracy. Edit it to sound like you. AI makes mistakes, sometimes confidently.

- **Ask your supervisor or the IT Manager if you are unsure.** When in doubt about whether a tool is approved or whether information is safe to use with AI, ask first.

**[4. WHAT YOU CANNOT DO]{.underline}**

- **Do not enter Confidential data into unapproved AI tools.** This includes personal information (names, Social Security numbers, addresses), financial records, health information, legal documents, and personnel files. See the AI Policy (Section 6.1) for the full list. If you are unsure whether something is Confidential, treat it as Confidential.

- **Do not use AI to make final decisions without human review.** AI can help you analyze and draft, but a person must review and approve any decision that affects residents, staff, vendors, or Organization operations.

- **Do not use unapproved AI tools for Organization work.** If a tool is not on the approved list in Section 5, do not use it for Organization business. This includes consumer versions of ChatGPT, Google Gemini, and similar services.

- **Do not tamper with or attempt to disable monitoring.** The Organization monitors AI usage on managed devices for security and compliance purposes. Do not bypass, disable, or interfere with these controls.

- **Do not use your Organization account with personal AI subscriptions.** Keep personal AI use separate from Organization work.

**Not Approved for Organization Work**

All other AI tools are unapproved, including but not limited to ChatGPT, Google Gemini, Claude, Perplexity, and similar third-party AI services. The free, personal, and consumer tiers of these tools are prohibited for Organization business under the AI Policy (Section 6.3). These tools have been identified and tagged in the Organization\'s cloud security environment.

- AI usage on Organization devices is monitored.

- Access to unapproved tools may be restricted in the future.

**Want to use a tool that is not on the approved list?** Submit a request to your Department Director, who forwards it to the IT Manager. The Organization will review the tool\'s security, privacy, and data-handling practices before making a decision. This list is updated as new tools are reviewed and approved.

**[6. HANDLING DISTRICT DATA WITH AI]{.underline}**

- Approved AI tools like Microsoft 365 Copilot work within the Organization\'s secure environment. Copilot respects existing permissions \-- it does not give you access to files or data you do not already have permission to see.

- **You are responsible for what you share, create, and store** \-- even when AI helps you produce it.

- When working with Confidential data (as defined in the Cyber Security Policy), only use approved AI tools that have been configured to handle that type of information.

- When in doubt, do not paste it. Ask the IT Manager first.

**[7. YOUR RESPONSIBILITIES FOR AI-GENERATED WORK]{.underline}**

When you use AI to help create work products, you own the result. That means:

- **Check the facts.** Verify AI-generated claims against reliable sources before sharing them.

- **Edit and refine.** Do not send AI-generated text as-is. Review it for accuracy, tone, and completeness.

- **Respect copyright.** Make sure AI output does not copy or closely mirror copyrighted material.

- **Meet quality standards.** AI-assisted work should meet the same standards as any other work product.

If you discover that an AI tool has produced harmful, inaccurate, or biased content that was acted upon, report it to your supervisor and the IT Manager.

**[8. MONITORING]{.underline}**

The Organization monitors AI usage on managed devices for security and compliance purposes \-- not to track individual employees. This monitoring helps the Organization:

- Detect if Confidential data is being shared with unapproved tools

- Understand how AI tools are being used across the organization

- Protect the Organization and the public from data exposure

**What this means for you:** If you are using approved tools on an Organization-managed device and following this guide, monitoring is routine and nothing to worry about.

**Limitations:** Monitoring depends on your device and browser setup. Personal devices, personal browser profiles, or unsupported platforms may not be fully covered. This does not mean those uses are permitted \-- the rules in this guide apply regardless of whether monitoring is in place.

**[9. WHAT HAPPENS IF YOU VIOLATE THIS GUIDE]{.underline}**

Violations are handled under the Organization\'s existing Employee Handbook and HR policies, the same as any other policy violation.
